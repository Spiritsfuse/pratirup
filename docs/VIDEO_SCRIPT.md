# Demonstration video script (target 22–24 min; the brief requires ≥ 20 min)

**Format:** screen recording plus voice, uploaded to YouTube as **unlisted**. Paste the link into the README.

**Before recording**
- Run `docker run -p 8000:8000 pratirup` or `python -m pratirup.cli serve`.
- Open http://localhost:8000.
- Have the deck (`docs/Pratirup_Presentation.pdf`) open in a second window.
- Use 1920×1080, browser zoom 110%, light theme.

**Speakers:** D = Dhruv, P = Pratyush, A = Anushika. Swap names freely; each section stands on its own.

---

### 1. Opening (0:00–1:30) · P · deck slides 1–2
- "We are Team Aflatoon from Scaler School of Technology. This is Pratirup, a glucose digital twin for type 2 diabetes care teams."
- Say what will be shown: a working twin, evaluated on patients it never saw, with every number generated by code in the public repo.
- Disclaimer up front: research prototype, not a medical device.

### 2. The problem (1:30–4:00) · A · slides 3–4
- **Burden:** ICMR-INDIAB-17: 101 million people with diabetes in India; only about a third reach the HbA1c target.
- **Current workflow:** CGM is spreading, but clinicians review it retrospectively (an AGP report at a visit), separately from labs and medications.
- **What already exists (be explicit):**
  - Dexcom predicts lows 20 min ahead; Roche SmartGuide gives a 2-hour forecast.
  - These are patient-facing, CGM-only and tied to one vendor.
  - "So forecasting is not our novelty."
- **Our angle:** a clinician-facing twin that uses the record, ranks a panel by imminent risk, explains itself and scores its own forecasts.

### 3. What makes it a twin (4:00–6:30) · D · slides 5–6 + architecture PDF
- Walk the state table: clinical context, Kalman glycaemic state, event context, personal profile, model trust, data quality.
- Walk the update loop on the architecture diagram:
  1. A reading arrives.
  2. The Kalman update runs.
  3. Past forecasts resolve.
  4. Features are rebuilt from the past only.
  5. Forecast, interval and risk are computed.
  6. The forecast goes into the ledger.
- Mention the parity test: the streaming twin equals the evaluated replay, reading by reading.

### 4. Data (6:30–8:30) · A · slide 7
- **Why ShanghaiT2DM:** the clinical record and the CGM, insulin, drug and diet logs belong to the same 100 patients. Fusion is real, not a join of strangers.
- **What we rejected:**
  - Synthea + unrelated CGM: different people.
  - UCI #34: type 1 diabetes, 1991 finger-prick data.
- **Honest gaps:** no Indian data, no genetic markers (shown as "not recorded"), Chinese hospital cohort.
- **External test:** CGMacros, US, free-living, different sensor.

### 5. Model and protocol (8:30–10:30) · D · slide 8
- **Baselines → ablation ladder → GRU.** Why LightGBM is served even though the GRU ties it: attributions, conformal intervals, no deep-learning runtime.
- **Protocol:**
  - Patient-level split.
  - Every choice made on development patients.
  - Test scored once, with a lock file.
  - Leakage test.

### 6. Live product demo (10:30–17:30) · P drives, D narrates
1. **Worklist** (1.5 min)
   - Set the clock to Day 3 07:30. Explain the priority tiers (consensus targets first, predicted risk next).
   - Point out the provenance banner and the AGP range bars.
   - Press play for a few ticks and show the ranking change.
2. **Patient twin** (2.5 min)
   - Open a "watch" patient, for example SH-2079.
   - Show the observed line, the forecast band at +30/+60/+120 (120 is exploratory), and the **past forecasts drawn against what actually happened**.
   - Read the "why" panel: "usual 60-min change at this time of day", "time since meal". Stress: associations, not causes.
   - Scrub back 1 hour and show that the twin only knows the past.
3. **Ledger** (1 min): this patient's MAE vs last value, interval coverage, by day. "The twin keeps score on itself."
4. **What-if** (1 min)
   - Remove the recent meal log and run. Show the forecast and risk change.
   - Read the causal disclaimer aloud.
5. **Live stream** (1 min)
   - Start a stream at 0.5 s per reading. Show the twin abstaining for the first 2 h, then forecasting, scoring and alerting live.
   - Optionally show `python scripts/device_stream.py` posting readings from a terminal.
6. **Record** (0.5 min): the FHIR R4 bundle, with "Genetic markers: not recorded".

### 7. Results (17:30–21:00) · A · Evidence page + slides 9–12
- **Forecast:** 60-min MAE 14.8 vs 20.1 mg/dL (−5.3, 95% CI −6.5 to −4.2); Parkes A+B ≥ 99.4%.
- **Intervals:** 80% intervals cover 81 / 81 / 84% at 30 / 60 / 120 min.
- **Alerts:** above 180 → 95.5% of episodes caught, 65% with ≥ 30 min warning, 1.5 false alerts per day; the rate-of-change rule warns ≥ 30 min ahead for only 48%.
- **External:** T2D 60-min MAE 16.9 vs 22.4, coverage 78%.
- **What did not help** (say this proudly; it shows rigour):
  - clinical-record fields beyond CGM and logs,
  - online self-correction,
  - Fitbit heart rate and activity.
- **Failure modes:** sensitivity to random sensor noise; about 8 alerts per day across three alert types; low-glucose alerts above budget.

### 8. Real world and next steps (21:00–23:00) · P · slides 13–14
- **Buyers to validate:** diabetes clinic chains; digital-therapeutics programmes already running CGM coaching.
- **Barriers:** Indian validation, SaMD classification (CDSCO), DPDP compliance, vendor data access, alert-burden design.
- **Next:**

  2. Noise-augmented training.
  3. A prospective pilot with an Indian clinic.
  4. ABDM FHIR integration.
